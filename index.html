Why a habit tracker should forgive you

Most habit apps are secretly built on punishment. You string together a streak, feel great for two weeks, then one bad day breaks the chain — the counter resets to 0, usually in red. Your brain reads that as failure, and the next day you avoid opening the app at all. That's not a willpower problem. That's a design problem.

The missed day is treated as the enemy. But the missed day is actually the most useful moment there is — it's where you find out what really gets in your way. Punishing it just teaches you to quit.

What this tracker does differently

  • One missed day never resets you. The "kind streak" survives a single gap — it only pauses, it doesn't crash to zero.
  • Missed days are neutral grey, never red. No colour designed to make you feel like you failed.
  • "Days shown up" only ever goes up. Your real progress — the total number of times you turned up — can never be taken away.
  • Comebacks are counted as wins. Returning after a gap is the actual skill of building a habit, so it gets its own number.

Made with ADHD brains in mind

If all-or-nothing streaks have made you quit five different apps, you're the reason this exists. There's nothing to set up, no notifications nagging you, and no penalty for a messy week. Open it, tap the day, move on. Consistency is built from coming back, not from never missing.

Questions

Is this habit tracker really free?

Yes — completely free, no signup, no watermark, no limits, no upsell wall. It runs entirely in your browser and always will.

Does my data get uploaded anywhere?

No. Every habit and every check-in is stored locally in your browser using localStorage. There is no server and no account, so nothing ever leaves your device. Clearing your browser data will erase it — use "Export my data" to keep a copy.

What is a "kind streak"?

It's a streak that survives one missed day. Counting back from today, it adds up the days you showed up — and if it hits a single gap, it steps over it once and keeps going. Only two missed days in a row will pause it. It's designed so one bad day can't wipe out weeks of progress.

Why is this good for ADHD?

All-or-nothing streaks punish the exact thing ADHD brains struggle with most: perfect consistency. When one miss resets everything to zero, the guilt makes you avoid the app entirely. A forgiving tracker keeps your progress visible after a slip, so coming back feels easy instead of shameful.
